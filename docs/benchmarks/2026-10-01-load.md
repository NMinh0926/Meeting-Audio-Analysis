# Load: 10 files uploaded at once (2026-10-01)

Worker with the GPU memory fix (`fd39cfe`), freshly started. 10 copies of `life_abroad_ep4_5min.mp3`
(4.8 MB, 5 min, 2 speakers) sent as 10 parallel `POST /api/v1/meetings` requests with curl.

## Upload and API

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| 10 parallel uploads (48 MB) | all `202`, 3.98–4.35 s each, 4.5 s for all |
| `GET /api/v1/meetings?limit=50` while the worker transcribes | 9–19 ms (10 calls) |
| `GET …/audio` with a 1 MB `Range` while the worker transcribes | 29–47 ms, `206` |

## Processing (single worker, one job at a time)

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Failed jobs | 0/10, every file found 2 speakers |
| First job (includes loading Whisper, pyannote, ECAPA, sentiment) | 110 s |
| Each following job | 71–72 s, flat across the run |
| All 10 done | 12 min 31 s after the upload (50 min of audio → ≈ 4× real time) |
| Queue wait of the last job | 11 min 20 s |

## What this means
- Jobs run one at a time on the single GPU, so a batch of N files finishes after roughly
  `N × time per file`, and the k-th file waits `(k − 1) × time per file` before it starts.
- Time per file: ~71 s for 5 minutes of audio (diarization has a fixed cost per file), ~0.106 × audio
  length for long recordings (`2026-10-01-gpu-memory.md`). One hour of audio ≈ 6.5 min.
- Capacity of this machine: about **4 h of 5-minute recordings** or **9 h of long recordings** per hour of
  processing. The queue grows (files wait longer and longer) only when more audio than that arrives per
  hour; uploads and the API stay responsive meanwhile because they do not touch the GPU.
- More throughput needs more GPUs (one worker each); two workers on this 4 GB card would not fit.
